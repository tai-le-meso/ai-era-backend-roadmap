What I learn
Red-teaming your own featureTry direct instruction injection, instruction-in-document, unicode tricks, and role confusion. Every success is a finding. This is the same exercise as 4B's threat modelling, applied to a new attack surface.
Defence in depthNo single mitigation stops injection. The layered defence: untrusted content delimited and labelled, output constrained by schema, values verified against your data, and the action authorised independently of what the model said.
What I do
  1. Run a full red-team session against your extraction feature.
  2. Log every successful injection and add a mitigation.
  3. Verify the layered defence: even a successful injection must not produce an unauthorised action.
  4. Commit the injection test suite.
What I conclude
  • The goal is not to prevent every injection but to ensure a successful injection cannot cause an unauthorised effect.
  • Authorisation must never depend on what the model said.
What I remember & apply
Authorise actions independently of model output → apply: the model proposes, your code checks permissions.
Layer defences: delimit, schema, verify, authorise → apply: no single layer is sufficient.
Keep the injection suite in CI → apply: prompt changes can reintroduce vulnerabilities.
