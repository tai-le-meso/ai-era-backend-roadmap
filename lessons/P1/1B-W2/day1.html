Learn I — Garbage collection: G1, ZGC, and the pause you can actually feel

JVM Internals, Concurrency & Performance · Phase 1 — Engineering Foundation · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
Why generational collection worksThe weak generational hypothesis: most objects die young. So collect the young generation frequently and cheaply by copying the few survivors, and collect the old generation rarely. Every mainstream JVM collector is built on this observation.
Roots, barriers and remembered setsGC starts from roots (stack locals, statics, JNI) and traces. To collect the young generation alone, the collector must know about old→young references — that is what the remembered set holds and what the write barrier maintains on every reference store. Barriers are the ongoing cost of GC that never appears in a pause metric.
G1's structureThe heap is divided into equal-size regions, each labelled eden, survivor, old or humongous. G1 collects a collection set chosen to fit a pause-time goal (-XX:MaxGCPauseMillis, default 200ms). Objects larger than half a region are humongous and allocated directly in old — a common source of surprise fragmentation.
Reading unified GC logs-Xlog:gc*:file=gc.log:time,uptime,level,tags. Each pause line gives cause, before/after sizes, and duration. The three numbers worth tracking: pause duration distribution, allocation rate, and promotion rate.
What I do
  1. Read the G1 chapter of the GC Tuning Guide.
  2. Enable -Xlog:gc* on the flagship and generate 30 minutes of sustained load with k6.
  3. Parse the log into a pause-duration distribution — do not just read the max.
  4. Write your prediction of how ZGC will differ before you run it.
What I conclude
  • Pause-time goal is a goal, not a guarantee — G1 adjusts the collection set to try to meet it and will miss under pressure.
  • Write barriers are throughput cost you pay continuously to make pauses short; low-pause collectors trade throughput for latency.
What I remember & apply
G1 default pause goal is 200ms via -XX:MaxGCPauseMillis → apply: set it deliberately to your latency SLO, not by default.
Objects > half a region are humongous, allocated in old → apply: large byte arrays can fragment old gen; consider region size -XX:G1HeapRegionSize.
-Xlog:gc* is the modern unified logging flag → apply: the old -XX:+PrintGCDetails flags are gone; use unified logging.
Track the pause distribution, not the max → apply: same p50/p95/p99 discipline as request latency.
