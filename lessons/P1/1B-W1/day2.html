What I learn
Reading a heap dumpjcmd <pid> GC.heap_dump then open in Eclipse MAT. The dominator tree shows what retains what; the leak suspects report is usually right. The number that matters is retained size, not shallow size.
JFR event typesAllocation profiling, GC pauses, thread stalls, lock contention, exceptions thrown, and IO. JFR is designed for always-on production use with ~1% overhead — that is its advantage over sampling profilers.
What I do
  1. Reproduce each of the three leaks under load and capture a heap dump for each.
  2. Open each dump in MAT and find the leak from the dominator tree — do not look at your own source first.
  3. Fix all three and confirm with a second dump.
  4. Start docs/perf/heap-analysis.md with the dominator-tree evidence for each.
What I conclude
  • Retained size is the number that identifies a leak; shallow size misleads.
  • Finding your own deliberately-planted leak from evidence alone is the rehearsal for finding an unknown one at 3am.
What I remember & apply
Retained size, not shallow size → apply: sort by retained in MAT's dominator tree first, always.
JFR is ~1% overhead and production-safe → apply: argue for always-on JFR in your services.
jcmd <pid> GC.heap_dump → apply: memorise this; it is the first command in a memory incident.
