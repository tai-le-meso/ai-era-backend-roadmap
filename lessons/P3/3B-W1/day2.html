Learn II → Design — 2PC, and why you probably can't use it

Distributed Transactions & Observability · Phase 3 — Distributed Systems · ~4.5h
Day 1Day 2 — Learn II → DesignDay 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
Recovering from coordinator failureThe recovery procedure: read the coordinator's durable decision log, find in-doubt transactions, and resolve each. Without a durable decision log written before sending commits, recovery is impossible — the coordinator cannot know what it decided.
Where 2PC is genuinely rightOne database with multiple schemas (use a single transaction instead). XA across a database and a JMS broker in a single deployment unit. Tightly coupled legacy systems with a shared transaction manager and local network. Note the pattern: all local, all controlled, all already jointly available.
What I do
  1. Kill the coordinator between prepare and commit; observe participants blocked in doubt.
  2. Inspect pg_prepared_xacts and pg_locks and confirm your prediction.
  3. Write and test the recovery procedure using the decision log.
  4. Document the whole experiment.
What I conclude
  • The durable decision log is the entire recovery mechanism; without it, 2PC has no recovery story.
  • Naming the narrow cases where 2PC is correct makes your rejection of it credible rather than dogmatic.
What I remember & apply
Coordinator must durably log its decision before sending commits → apply: otherwise recovery is impossible.
2PC fits local, controlled, jointly-available resources → apply: name these cases when someone proposes it, then explain why yours is not one.
