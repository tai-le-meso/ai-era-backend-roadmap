Learn I — 2PC, and why you probably can't use it

Distributed Transactions & Observability · Phase 3 — Distributed Systems · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
Two-phase commitPhase 1: the coordinator asks every participant to prepare; each durably promises it can commit and holds its locks. Phase 2: if all voted yes, the coordinator tells everyone to commit. It gives genuine atomicity across resources — which is why it is attractive and why it keeps being proposed.
The blocking problemIf the coordinator dies after prepare and before commit, participants are in doubt: they hold locks and cannot decide alone, because committing might contradict a peer and aborting might contradict a commit decision already sent elsewhere. They block until the coordinator returns. In PostgreSQL an orphaned prepared transaction holds locks and blocks vacuum indefinitely.
Why 2PC does not fit microservicesIt requires all participants to support the protocol (HTTP and Kafka do not), it holds locks across network round trips, and it converts independent availability into joint availability — the system is available only if every participant is. That is the opposite of the property microservices exist to provide.
FLP, intuitivelyIn an asynchronous system with even one possible crash failure, no deterministic protocol can guarantee consensus in bounded time. This is why every practical system uses timeouts and accepts a small probability of incorrect failure suspicion.
What I do
  1. Read PostgreSQL's PREPARE TRANSACTION docs including the warnings.
  2. Implement a naive 2PC coordinator across two local resources.
  3. Write down what an in-doubt participant can and cannot decide alone.
  4. Predict what pg_locks will show for an orphaned prepared transaction.
What I conclude
  • 2PC is not wrong; it is unavailable. That is a different criticism and a more precise one.
  • The blocking window is unbounded, which is what makes it unacceptable for user-facing services.
What I remember & apply
2PC: prepare (promise + hold locks) then commit → apply: only viable where all participants support it and latency is local.
Coordinator failure leaves participants in doubt, holding locks → apply: this is the disqualifying property, not slowness.
Orphaned prepared transactions block vacuum forever → apply: monitor pg_prepared_xacts if you enable 2PC at all.
FLP: no bounded-time consensus with async + crash → apply: every timeout is an engineering compromise, not a bug.
