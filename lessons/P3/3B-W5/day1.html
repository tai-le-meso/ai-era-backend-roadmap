Learn I — Logging & distributed tracing

Distributed Transactions & Observability · Phase 3 — Distributed Systems · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
Structured loggingJSON output means fields are queryable rather than regex-extracted. Levels are a contract: ERROR means someone must act, WARN means it is worth knowing, INFO is the story of the request, DEBUG is for development. An ERROR that nobody acts on trains everyone to ignore ERRORs.
What must never be loggedPII, card numbers (PCI: never store PAN in logs), CVV under any circumstances, tokens, passwords, full request bodies containing any of these. This is a compliance obligation in Phase 5, and log redaction is far easier to build now than to retrofit.
Trace, span, and contextA trace is one end-to-end operation; a span is one unit of work within it; context carries trace id, span id and flags across boundaries. The W3C traceparent header is the interop format — version-traceid-spanid-flags.
Propagation across KafkaHTTP propagation is automatic in most instrumentations. Kafka is not: you must inject the context into record headers on produce and extract on consume. Nearly every team's trace breaks at exactly this hop, so it is the one worth testing explicitly.
What I do
  1. Read the OTel context-propagation concepts page and the W3C traceparent format.
  2. Add OpenTelemetry to the flagship with auto-instrumentation for HTTP and JDBC.
  3. Add trace id and span id to every log line via MDC.
  4. List every field in your logs that must be redacted.
What I conclude
  • Log levels are a contract with the on-call engineer; violating it produces alert fatigue.
  • The Kafka hop is where distributed traces almost always break.
What I remember & apply
traceparent: version-traceid-spanid-flags → apply: the W3C format; use it rather than a vendor header.
Inject/extract trace context in Kafka record headers manually → apply: auto-instrumentation usually does not cover it.
Never log PAN, CVV, tokens or passwords → apply: build redaction now; Phase 5 makes it a compliance requirement.
ERROR means someone must act → apply: audit your ERROR logs; demote the ones nobody acts on.
