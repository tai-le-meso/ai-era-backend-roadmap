What I learn
Reducing a cost driver measurablyPick the top driver and reduce it with a measurement before and after. Common wins: metric cardinality reduction, right-sizing over-provisioned instances, better cache hit rates, and reducing cross-AZ traffic.
Sustainability and efficiencyEfficiency is now an architecture characteristic in its own right — fewer wasted cycles means lower cost and lower carbon. They usually align, which makes the argument easy.
Complexity as a costEach additional component costs ongoing attention: upgrades, CVEs, on-call knowledge, and onboarding time. Counting components is a crude but useful complexity metric.
What I do
  1. Reduce the top cost driver and measure the saving.
  2. Count the flagship's components and estimate the annual attention cost of each.
  3. Build the quantified technical-debt register.
  4. Prioritise the register by interest, not by annoyance.
What I conclude
  • Prioritising debt by interest rather than by irritation is what makes the register credible to non-engineers.
  • Counting components is crude and correlates well with real operational burden.
What I remember & apply
Reduce the top cost driver with before/after measurement → apply: one measured reduction beats five proposals.
Count components as a complexity proxy → apply: each one costs ongoing attention.
Prioritise debt by interest → apply: the most annoying item is rarely the most expensive.
