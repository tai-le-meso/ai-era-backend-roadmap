Learn I — Architecture Decision Records as a habit

Architecture Decision-Making, Communication & Capstone · Phase 8 — Solution Architecture · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
The six sectionsContext (what problem, what forces). Constraints (what limits the options). Options (what was considered — including the ones rejected, fairly described). Decision (what was chosen). Trade-offs (what is given up). Consequences (what happens operationally). Skipping Options is the most common failure and it makes the ADR read as justification.
Recording assumptions and their expiry'We assume fewer than 100 tenants' is an assumption with an expiry date. Recording it means the ADR can be revisited when the assumption breaks, rather than quietly becoming wrong.
Superseding versus amendingAn ADR is immutable once accepted. A changed decision gets a new ADR that supersedes the old one, and the old one stays — because the history is the value. Amending in place destroys the record of how thinking changed.
ADRs as onboarding materialA new engineer reading the ADR set in order learns why the system is the way it is in an afternoon. That is the highest-return use of the format and the reason to keep the rejected options in.
What I do
  1. Read the ADR templates and pick the format you will standardise on.
  2. Audit all your existing ADRs (0001–0010+) against the six sections.
  3. Identify the three weakest and note what is missing from each.
  4. List five significant decisions in the flagship that have no ADR.
What I conclude
  • The Options section is what makes an ADR analysis rather than justification.
  • Assumptions with expiry dates are what make an ADR revisitable rather than stale.
What I remember & apply
Six sections: context, constraints, options, decision, trade-offs, consequences → apply: never skip Options.
Record assumptions with their expiry conditions → apply: 'we assume <100 tenants' can then be revisited.
Supersede, never amend → apply: the history of changed thinking is the value.
ADRs are the best onboarding material you can write → apply: keep the rejected options in.
