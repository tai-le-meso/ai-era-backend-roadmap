What I learn
Why you never edit a ledger entryAn UPDATE destroys three things at once: the audit trail (the wrong value and who fixed it vanish), reproducibility (yesterday's reports can no longer be regenerated — trial balances, statements and regulatory filings silently disagree with the new data), and downstream consistency (any system that already consumed the old value now diverges forever). A reversal preserves all three: the error, the correction, and the timeline are all facts.
Value date vs booking date — bitemporalityBooking date = when the entry hit the ledger (record time, append-only). Value date = when it economically applies (actual time — interest accrues from here), and it can be backdated. This is Fowler's bitemporal model: 'balance on date D as known at date T' needs both axes; a backdated entry changes the answer for (D, now) but never for (D, T<posting).
Reversal linkage as first-class datareverses_entry_id on the compensating entry (and reversal_reason) makes reversals queryable: net-of-reversals views, reversal-rate metrics, and the auditor's 'show me the correction chain' become one join instead of forensic archaeology.
What I do
  1. Read Fowler's bitemporal article; define your two time columns (booking_at, value_date) and write the as-of query in SQL: balance on D as known on T = sum of lines with value_date ≤ D and booking_at ≤ T.
  2. Re-read the adjustment/reversal sections of Fowler's accounting paper; decide reverse-and-repost vs difference-posting as your adjustment strategy and document why.
  3. Add reverses_entry_id + reason to the schema with a constraint that a reversal balances its target.
  4. Write test cases for backdating: post on D+2 valued D, then assert both as-of answers.
What I conclude
  • Bitemporality is what lets an append-only ledger still 'fix the past': you change what is true about D without changing what was known at T.
  • Reverse-and-repost is cleaner for audit (two symmetric facts) than difference-posting, at the cost of more entries — pick one and be consistent.
  • Every correction needs a reason code; 'adjustment' with no why is an audit finding.
What I remember & apply
Booking date = record time (append-only); value date = actual/economic time (backdatable) → apply: interest calculation and the as-of query in day 3.
As-of-T queries filter on booking_at ≤ T — reports become reproducible forever → apply: regulator report in 5A-W7.
Reversal entries carry reverses_entry_id + reason and must exactly balance the original → apply: schema migration today.
UPDATE on a ledger row breaks reproducibility of every already-issued report → apply: the day-4 'why the hotfix is wrong' write-up.
