What I learn
Risk scoring: rules vs modelsRule engines (amount thresholds, velocity, geo/channel mismatch, list hits) are explainable, auditable, and fast to change — the right start. ML models catch subtler patterns but need training data, monitoring, and explainability work. Either way the decision must be logged with its inputs and reasons: 'score 82 because rules R3, R7 fired' — regulators and disputes both demand the why.
False positives have a priceA false decline costs the sale, support cost, and customer trust (declined customers churn); a false accept costs the fraud amount plus fees. Thresholds are therefore a business optimisation, not a security maximisation — the risk team tunes the trade-off, and your job is making outcomes measurable (decision logs joined to eventual fraud/chargeback truth).
SCA, exemptions, and 3DSPSD2 SCA (per RTS 2018/389) requires two of knowledge/possession/inherence for electronic payments, with exemptions: low-value (Art. 16), transaction-risk-analysis under fraud-rate thresholds (Art. 18), trusted beneficiaries (Art. 13), recurring same-amount (Art. 14). Step-up authentication = invoking the challenge only when risk demands: EMV 3DS implements this for cards — frictionless flow (data-only authentication) vs challenge flow. Design your risk decision to output allow / step-up / deny, not just allow/deny.
What I do
  1. Read SCA RTS Articles 10–18; write one line per exemption: condition, who takes liability, design implication.
  2. Read the EMVCo 3DS page; diagram frictionless vs challenge flow and where your risk decision would trigger a challenge.
  3. Design the rule-based risk scorer: ordered rules, weights, threshold bands mapping to allow/step-up/deny, and the decision-audit record (inputs, fired rules, score, decision, timestamp).
  4. Define 3 metrics you would track to tune thresholds (decline rate, fraud rate on accepted, step-up conversion).
What I conclude
  • allow/step-up/deny beats allow/deny: step-up converts uncertainty into evidence instead of lost sales.
  • An unexplainable risk decision is a compliance and support liability regardless of its accuracy — log inputs and reasons, always.
  • Exemptions exist because friction has a measurable cost; SCA is an optimisation problem the regulation itself acknowledges.
What I remember & apply
SCA = 2 of knowledge/possession/inherence; key exemptions: low-value, TRA, trusted beneficiary, recurring → apply: risk-decision outputs on day 3.
3DS frictionless vs challenge = risk-based step-up in the card world → apply: step-up hook in the payment saga.
Every risk decision logs inputs + fired rules + score + outcome → apply: decision-audit table and the 5A-W7 audit API.
False-positive cost is a business number; make it measurable → apply: decision-log ↔ fraud-outcome join in reporting.
