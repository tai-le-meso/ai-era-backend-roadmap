What I learn
Auditing an AI-designed limits checkAsk AI to design the limits check, then attack its design with your day-3 test scenario. Typical flaws: check in application code with no locking, check and reserve in separate transactions, cache-based counters (race by construction), or per-instance in-memory state that shards the truth. The exercise: name the interleaving that breaks each.
Where each control must liveSummary you should now own: limit integrity → database transaction; risk decision → before limits, logged; fee terms → versioned data; step-up → risk outcome, not an afterthought; false-positive tuning → measured business trade-off. Each control has exactly one correct home, dictated by 2A isolation semantics and 4A aggregate boundaries.
What I do
  1. Run the drill: get AI's limits design, break it with a concrete interleaving, and document the flaw class.
  2. Run your concurrent-breach test against your own implementation one more time under higher contention (10 threads).
  3. Commit deliverables: versioned fee engine + race-proof limits engine + risk decision log.
  4. Self-check: state, in three sentences, where the limit check happens and why it cannot be raced.
What I conclude
  • 'Where can this be raced?' is the review question for every check-then-act in the codebase — you now have the tooling to answer it with a test.
  • Controls have architectural homes; a correct check in the wrong layer is still a bug.
What I remember & apply
AI limit designs default to app-layer check-then-act; demand the transactional boundary → apply: code-review checklist.
In-memory or cache counters cannot enforce financial limits across instances → apply: reject in design review.
The proof of race-freedom is a repeated barrier test, not an argument → apply: keep it in CI forever.
