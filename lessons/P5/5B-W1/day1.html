What I learn
Card payments: four parties, three phasesIssuer (cardholder's bank), acquirer (merchant's bank), scheme (network routing and rules), merchant. Authorisation: real-time yes/no + hold on the cardholder's available balance. Clearing: batch exchange of the actual transaction records. Settlement: net interbank movement, typically T+1. Interchange flows acquirer→issuer; the merchant is paid net of it — which is why a card payment is 'approved' instantly but not final for days (chargebacks extend that for months).
Batch rails: ACH and SEPA SCTPayments accumulate into files, exchanged at scheduled clearing cycles with cut-off times; settlement nets across the batch. Returns are first-class: ACH R-codes and SEPA R-transactions (reject before settlement, return after) arrive DAYS later — so 'sent' is not 'done', and your ledger must represent settled-then-returned.
RTGS: Fedwire and T2Real-time gross settlement: each payment settles individually and immediately in central bank money — per the Fed, 'immediate, final, and irrevocable once processed'. No returns mechanism at the scheme level: recovering a mistaken wire is a bilateral request, not a right. Used for large-value/time-critical payments; the cost is liquidity (gross, not netted).
Instant rails: SCT Inst and peersSEPA Instant: sub-10-second end-to-end, 24/7/365, with finality on completion; the Instant Payments Regulation (EU 2024/886) mandates EEA PSP support. Instant means instant finality too — fraud checks must happen pre-send, not during a batch window (UK FPS and others are the regional analogues).
What I do
  1. Read the Fedwire page, ECB T2 page, EPC SCT and SCT Inst pages, and the Nacha guide's network overview.
  2. Build the finality/returns matrix: rail × (speed, finality moment, revocability, return window+mechanism, operating calendar, netting vs gross).
  3. For a card purchase, write the timeline auth→clearing→settlement→chargeback-window with who holds risk at each stage.
  4. Trace one real payment you made recently and place it on the matrix.
What I conclude
  • Rails differ on exactly three design-relevant axes: when is it final, what can come back, and when does it run — everything else is plumbing detail.
  • Card 'approved' is a credit decision, not money movement; settlement and chargebacks come later — status models must not conflate them.
  • RTGS finality is a feature AND a hazard: no take-backs means pre-send controls carry all the weight.
What I remember & apply
Card: authorisation (instant hold) → clearing (batch records) → settlement (net T+1) → apply: card adapter states and the 5A-W3 hold model.
ACH/SEPA returns arrive days post-settlement; model settled→returned → apply: ledger + state machine in 5B-W2.
Fedwire/T2: final and irrevocable on processing, no scheme returns → apply: extra confirmation/controls on the wire adapter path.
SCT Inst: ≤10s, 24/7, instant finality (EU 2024/886 mandates support) → apply: pre-send fraud check placement in 5B-W3.
