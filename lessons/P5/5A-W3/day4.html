What I learn
Arguing the designThe strongest attacks on snapshot+delta: snapshot job lag makes delta scans grow unbounded; a bug that writes a wrong snapshot poisons reads until rebuild; per-balance-type snapshots can disagree if not written atomically; and holds live outside the ledger, so 'available' correctness depends on a second system's hygiene. Have written answers: lag alerting, rebuild cadence, atomic multi-type snapshot writes, hold-expiry monitoring.
Explaining two balances to a PMScript: 'The app shows what you can spend (available) and what you own (booked). A card hold reduces the first immediately but touches the second only at capture, days later. Both numbers are correct answers to different questions.'
What I do
  1. Run the drill: ask AI for the strongest argument against your balance strategy; force specifics (snapshot lag, poisoned snapshot, hold drift) and write your rebuttal or design change for each.
  2. Fix at least one real weakness the drill exposed (e.g. add snapshot-lag metric + alert).
  3. Commit deliverables: three balance types, rebuild-and-verify job, hot-account benchmark numbers.
  4. Write the two-balances PM explanation into the docs and rehearse it once.
What I conclude
  • Every derived-data design owes you two artifacts: a verifier (rebuild job) and a lag/health metric — without them 'fast and correct' is just 'fast'.
  • Generic AI critiques ('eventual consistency is hard') are noise; forcing specificity against your actual schema is where the value is.
What I remember & apply
Snapshot lag must be a monitored metric with an alert threshold → apply: ops dashboard for the flagship.
Balance-type snapshots must be written atomically together → apply: snapshot writer implementation.
PM script: available = spendable now, booked = owned now → apply: customer-status wording in 5B-W2's UNKNOWN state.
