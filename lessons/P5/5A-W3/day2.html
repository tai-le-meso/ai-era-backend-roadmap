What I learn
Three derivation strategiesCompute-from-entries: SUM over ledger_line — always correct, O(entries), too slow for hot reads. Running-balance column: fast but updated in place, so a race, crash or bug drifts it with no detection — a correctness hazard because the error is silent and compounding. Snapshot+delta: persist balance as of entry N (or time T), answer reads as snapshot + SUM(entries after N) — fast, and always recomputable from scratch.
Snapshot cadence and rebuildCadence trades read latency (longer delta scans) against write/storage cost of snapshotting. Rebuild-from-scratch must exist as a runnable command: recompute every balance from the journal and assert equality with snapshots — it is both your corruption detector and your disaster recovery.
The hot-account problemEvery posting touching one account (fee income, FBO, suspense) serialises on that account's row lock or version check, capping TPS regardless of hardware. Mitigations: don't lock on record-only accounts (Modern Treasury's recording mode), split the hot account into N sub-accounts rolled up on read, or batch postings. Under READ COMMITTED, two concurrent check-then-post flows can both pass a naive balance check — you need row locking (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE), a serializable transaction, or a version/condition CAS.
Recording vs authorizing writesAuthorizing writes (must not overdraw) need read-after-write consistency and locking; recording writes (mirroring an external fact that already happened) must never be rejected for balance reasons — externally settled money doesn't stop being real because your check failed.
What I do
  1. Read Scale-a-Ledger Part IV; write one paragraph on version locking vs balance-condition locking and which your design uses.
  2. Re-read PostgreSQL transaction isolation for READ COMMITTED anomalies; write the exact race that corrupts a naive check-then-insert balance flow.
  3. Design the snapshot table (account_id, as_of_entry_seq, balance per balance-type) and choose a cadence with a written justification.
  4. Design the rebuild command and the hot-account mitigation you will benchmark (pick one deliberately).
What I conclude
  • Running-balance columns fail silently — the drift is only discovered at audit or customer complaint; snapshot+delta fails loudly via the rebuild-verify job.
  • Correctness first, then speed: snapshot+delta is exactly compute-from-entries with a cache, so it inherits correctness while buying latency.
  • Distinguish authorizing from recording writes early; applying authorization locking to recorded external facts causes data loss.
What I remember & apply
Snapshot+delta = persisted balance at entry N + sum of deltas since N; rebuildable from zero → apply: day-3 implementation and the 5A-W7 point-in-time report.
Naive check-then-post races under READ COMMITTED; use FOR UPDATE, SERIALIZABLE, or CAS on account version → apply: proof of race-free limits in 5B-W3.
Hot account fixes: sub-account sharding, posting batching, or lock-free recording mode → apply: day-3 benchmark design.
Never reject a recording write for insufficient balance — the money already moved externally → apply: recon-driven postings in 5A-W6.
