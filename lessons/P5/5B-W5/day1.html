What I learn
GDPR rights as system capabilitiesAccess (Art. 15) = produce all personal data you hold on a subject — you need to KNOW where it all is (the W4 classification table is the prerequisite). Rectification (16) = correct it. Erasure (17) = delete it — subject to exemptions. Portability (20) = machine-readable export. Each right has a one-month clock, so each needs a workflow with an owner, not an ad-hoc engineering scramble per request.
The erasure-vs-ledger conflict, preciselyArticle 17 grants erasure; Article 17(3)(b) disapplies it where processing is necessary to comply with a legal obligation — and financial record-keeping (AML record retention, accounting law) IS such an obligation. So the real design question is never 'delete the ledger?' (no) but 'separate what must be retained (financial facts, under legal basis) from what can be erased (identifying data beyond the retention requirement), and erase that properly'.
Resolution strategiesCrypto-shredding: encrypt each subject's personal data with a per-subject key; erasure = destroy the key, ciphertext remains but is unreadable. Pseudonymisation: replace identifiers with irreversible tokens, keep financial facts linked to the token. Legal-basis retention: explicitly document what you keep, under which law, for how long — and erase when the clock expires. Real systems combine all three, driven by the classification table.
What I do
  1. Read GDPR Articles 15–20 and Article 17(3); quote 17(3)(b) verbatim into your notes.
  2. Read the ICO erasure guidance; list the exemptions and the response-clock rules.
  3. For every PII field in the W4 classification table, decide: erasable on request / retained under legal basis until date X / pseudonymise. Record the legal basis for each retention.
  4. Sketch the data-subject-request workflow: intake, identity verification, one-month clock, execution, evidence of completion.
What I conclude
  • The 'immutable ledger vs right to erasure' conflict is resolved by the regulation itself: legal-obligation retention is an explicit exemption — the engineering task is cleanly separating retained facts from erasable identity.
  • Erasure is a per-field decision recorded in the classification table, not a per-customer DELETE statement.
  • Rights are workflows with deadlines and evidence, so they need the same engineering as any product feature.
What I remember & apply
Art. 17(3)(b): erasure yields to legal-obligation processing — financial retention qualifies → apply: the DPO argument on day 4.
Every retained personal-data field carries a documented legal basis and an expiry → apply: classification-table columns added today.
Data-subject requests have a one-month clock → apply: workflow with owner and SLA in the flagship.
Access right presupposes a complete data inventory → apply: the W4 table is that inventory; keep it true.
