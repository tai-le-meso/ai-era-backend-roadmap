What I learn
KYC / CDD / EDD as data and workflowKnow Your Customer = identity verification at onboarding (documents, liveness, registry checks); Customer Due Diligence = understanding who they are and expected activity, including beneficial ownership for entities (who ultimately owns/controls ≥25% — a graph, not a field); Enhanced DD = deeper checks for higher-risk customers (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions). System translation: a KYC status state machine (unverified → pending → verified → review_due → blocked) gating account capabilities, evidence storage with retention, periodic-review scheduling, and a risk-rating field that drives EDD triggers.
AML transaction monitoringDetecting patterns after money moves: structuring/smurfing (many transactions under reporting thresholds), layering (rapid movement across accounts to obscure origin), unusual-for-profile activity. System translation: rules/models over transaction streams producing ALERTS, triaged into CASES with an investigation workflow, ending in dismissal (documented) or a SAR/STR filing to the financial-intelligence unit.
Tipping-off: a system requirementIt is an offence to reveal that a SAR was filed or an investigation is underway. Design consequences: case data is need-to-know (separate authorisation domain), customer-facing surfaces must never leak monitoring state ('your account is under review' emails, status fields in APIs, even distinguishable error behaviour), and support staff tooling shows a sanitised view.
What I do
  1. Read the FATF Recommendations overview and the CDD/record-keeping/reporting items; write the systems-requirements list each implies (data kept, for how long, produced to whom).
  2. Design the KYC status state machine and the capability-gating matrix (which statuses allow open/receive/send/withdraw).
  3. Design the beneficial-ownership model as a graph (Party→ownership-edge→Party with percentages) and the ≥25% traversal query.
  4. Design the alert→case→outcome (dismiss/SAR) workflow with need-to-know access boundaries.
What I conclude
  • Compliance regimes are, to an engineer, data-retention + workflow + access-control specifications — read them that way and they stop being scary.
  • KYC gates capabilities, not existence: an account can exist but do nothing until verified — model it as capability flags driven by status.
  • Tipping-off prohibition is an information-flow constraint on your whole surface area, including error messages.
What I remember & apply
KYC status is a state machine gating capabilities per status → apply: day-3 compliance module.
Structuring = staying under thresholds; detect with windowed aggregation across accounts → apply: the monitoring rule you build day 3.
SAR/STR case data: need-to-know access, nothing leaks to customer-facing surfaces → apply: authorisation domain + log audit on day 4.
Beneficial ownership is a graph query (≥25% ultimate control) → apply: entity-customer onboarding model.
