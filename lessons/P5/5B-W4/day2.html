What I learn
Sanctions screening mechanicsLists (OFAC SDN, UN, EU consolidated) contain names with aliases, weak AKAs, partial DOBs and transliteration variants — exact matching misses real hits, so screening is fuzzy (normalisation, phonetics, edit distance, token reordering for name-order differences) and therefore produces false positives BY DESIGN. Screen at onboarding, on list updates (re-screen the book), and per-transaction for payment parties. Blocking (freeze the asset — OFAC-style) vs rejecting (refuse and return) are different obligations with different ledger outcomes — blocked funds sit in a dedicated blocked-funds account, not limbo.
PCI DSS at design levelScope = every system that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data (CHD) — the cardholder data environment (CDE). Never storable, even encrypted, after authorisation: full track data, CVV2/CVC2, PIN/PIN block (sensitive authentication data). PAN storable only rendered unreadable (strong crypto/truncation/hashing). Tokenisation swaps PAN for a token so most systems never touch CHD — the single best scope-reduction move: scope drives audit cost, so 'keep card data out of our systems entirely' is an architecture goal.
Basel and ISO 20022 as compliance substrateBasel sets capital/liquidity/risk standards — you won't implement them, but they explain why data quality, lineage (BCBS 239) and reporting infrastructure get funded. ISO 20022's structured fields (LEIs, structured names/addresses, purpose codes) make screening and regulatory reporting tractable — structured data is itself a compliance feature.
What I do
  1. Browse real SDN entries; collect 5 examples of aliases/weak AKAs/transliteration that would defeat exact matching.
  2. Download PCI DSS v4.x from the PCI SSC library; read Requirement 3 and write the never-store list plus the PAN-rendering options in your own words.
  3. Design the screening port: normalise → candidate-generate → score → threshold into hit/review/clear, with a false-positive review queue and full decision logging (list version screened against!).
  4. Design tokenisation for the flagship's card-like data: token vault boundary, what leaves the CDE, what your logs may contain.
What I conclude
  • Sanctions screening trades false positives for false negatives deliberately — a screening system without a review queue is either missing hits or drowning ops.
  • CVV is never storable after authorisation, full stop — 'encrypted' does not rescue it; this is the most cited PCI failure.
  • Recording the list version each screen ran against is what makes 'we screened them' provable later.
  • Structured data (ISO 20022) quietly does compliance work: better screening input, better reporting output.
What I remember & apply
Never store post-auth: CVV/CVC, full track data, PIN blocks — encryption doesn't make it legal → apply: day-3 tokenisation + day-4 log audit; the week's check question.
Tokenisation shrinks PCI scope; scope = audit surface = cost → apply: architecture decision record for the flagship.
Screen at onboarding + on list updates + per transaction; log list version per decision → apply: screening port implementation.
Blocking freezes funds (dedicated ledger account); rejecting returns them → apply: ledger entries for screening outcomes.
