What I learn
Currency-aware Money designMoney = (BigDecimal amount normalised to the currency's ISO 4217 minor-unit scale, CurrencyCode). add/subtract require equal currencies and throw otherwise; multiply by conversion is forbidden without an explicit FxConversion object. JSR 354 formalises this as MonetaryAmount + CurrencyUnit with MonetaryOperator for rounding — mirror its shape even if you hand-roll.
Allocation by largest remainderSplitting 100.00 into 3 by division gives 33.33×3 = 99.99 — one cent vanishes. Largest-remainder: compute the floor share in minor units (3333), distribute the remainder cents (1) one-by-one to the parties with the largest fractional remainders (or deterministically by index). Invariant: sum(parts) == original exactly, each part differs by at most one minor unit.
FX with provenanceA rate is (base, quote, bid/ask or mid, source, timestamp, validity window). Conversion must record which rate it used — auditors and customer disputes require reproducing the exact number later. Bid/ask spread means customer-sell and customer-buy use different rates; triangulation (EUR→USD→JPY) multiplies rates and compounds rounding, so round only at the final step and mind the cross-rate convention.
Never mix currencies in one operationThe type system should make money(EUR).add(money(USD)) uncompilable or throwing; a multi-currency account is N single-currency positions, not one summed number. Any 'total' across currencies is a report-time FX projection, clearly labelled with its rate date.
What I do
  1. Read Fowler's Money pattern and the JavaMoney site; write 5 bullet comparison of JSR 354's design vs your planned Money.
  2. Look up minor units for USD, JPY, BHD, CHF on the ISO 4217 page; encode them in a CurrencyCode enum/table.
  3. Work largest-remainder by hand: 100.00/3, 0.05/2, 10.00 split 30/30/40 weights; write expected outputs as test cases.
  4. Design the FxConversion record (rate, source, timestamp, validity) and the Money API signatures including allocate(int n) and allocate(int[] weights).
What I conclude
  • Allocation replaces division everywhere money is split — fees, taxes, instalments, revenue share.
  • An FX rate without source and timestamp is not auditable data, it is a rumour.
  • Reuse JSR 354's vocabulary (MonetaryAmount, CurrencyUnit, rounding operator) even in a hand-rolled type — future readers know the standard.
What I remember & apply
Largest-remainder allocation: floor shares in minor units, hand out remainder cents deterministically; sum is exact → apply: day-3 allocate() + property test.
JPY scale 0, BHD 3, most currencies 2 — scale comes from ISO 4217, never hardcode 2 → apply: Money normalisation and formatting.
FxConversion carries rate+source+timestamp+window; conversions store which rate they used → apply: multi-currency accounts on day 3 and audit answers in 5A-W7.
Cross-currency arithmetic must not compile/must throw → apply: Money.add precondition + ArchUnit rule.
