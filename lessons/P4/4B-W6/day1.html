Learn I — Design under constraints + consolidation

System Design & Architecture Practice · Phase 4 — DDD & Architecture · ~4.5h

What I learn
Designing under real constraintsLegacy systems you cannot change, a fixed budget, three engineers, a regulator requiring on-premise deployment, data residency rules. Constraints are not obstacles to a good design — they are the definition of which designs are good.
Build vs buyBuy when it is a generic subdomain, when the vendor's version is better than what you would build, and when the integration cost is lower than the build cost. Build when it is core, when the vendor's model fights yours, or when lock-in is unacceptable. Note this maps directly onto 4A-W1's classification.
Architecture characteristics are a budgetPerformance, scalability, availability, security, maintainability, testability, cost. You cannot maximise all of them; picking the top three explicitly means the others are consciously traded rather than accidentally lost.
Fitness functionsAutomated tests for architecture characteristics: ArchUnit rules for structure, a performance regression gate from your 1B baseline, a dependency-freshness check, a cyclomatic-complexity ceiling. They make architecture continuously verified rather than periodically reviewed.
What I do
  1. Do design drill 9 — core banking / ledger — timed. This is a dry run for Phase 5.
  2. Do design drill 10 — your own choice under a hard constraint (on-premise, 3 engineers, 6 months).
  3. Pick the flagship's top three architecture characteristics and write what you are trading away.
  4. List the fitness functions that would protect those three.
What I conclude
  • Constraints make design decisions; an unconstrained design problem has no wrong answers and teaches little.
  • Naming the top three characteristics forces the trade-offs into the open where they can be reviewed.
What I remember & apply
Build vs buy maps onto core/supporting/generic → apply: the 4A classification answers most build-vs-buy questions.
Pick top three architecture characteristics explicitly → apply: the rest are traded; say which.
Fitness functions make architecture continuously verified → apply: ArchUnit + perf gate + dependency check in CI.
Design under constraint is the realistic exercise → apply: add a constraint to every practice drill.
