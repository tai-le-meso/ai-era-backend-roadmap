Learn I — Strategic DDD: domains and subdomains

Domain-Driven Design · Phase 4 — DDD & Architecture · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
Domain, subdomain, and why the split mattersThe domain is the whole problem space your business operates in. Subdomains are coherent parts of it. The split matters because different subdomains deserve different levels of investment, and treating them uniformly is how teams spend their best engineers on commodity work.
Core, supporting, genericCore: what differentiates you commercially — build it, invest your strongest people, accept complexity. Supporting: necessary but not differentiating — build it simply, do not gold-plate. Generic: solved problems (auth, notifications, document storage) — buy, or use an off-the-shelf component.
The cost of misclassificationBuilding a generic subdomain in-house wastes years. Buying a core subdomain outsources your competitive advantage and usually cannot be undone. This is the most expensive category of architecture mistake because it is invisible in code review.
Ubiquitous language and how it diesOne language per bounded context, used identically by domain experts, code, tests and documentation. It dies when engineers translate — when the domain expert says 'settlement' and the code says processTransactionBatch. Every translation is a place bugs hide.
What I do
  1. Read the three Fowler articles — they are short.
  2. List every subdomain in the flagship banking platform.
  3. Classify each as core, supporting, or generic, with one sentence of justification.
  4. Do the same honestly for your actual employer's product.
What I conclude
  • Classification is a business judgment expressed in architecture; getting it wrong costs years, not sprints.
  • Translation between domain language and code language is where requirements get lost.
  • For a bank, payment routing and risk decisioning are usually core; KYC document capture is usually generic.
What I remember & apply
Core = differentiating, supporting = necessary, generic = buy → apply: check where your team's strongest engineers currently work.
Never build a generic subdomain in-house → apply: auth, notification delivery, document storage are almost always buy.
Never buy a core subdomain → apply: if it is your advantage, owning it is the point.
Ubiquitous language dies at translation points → apply: rename one class this week to match what the domain expert says.
