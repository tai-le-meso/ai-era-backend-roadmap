What I learn
Measuring defect categoriesTrack review findings by category: missing edge cases, wrong error handling, concurrency issues, convention violations, security gaps. The distribution tells you what to review most carefully, and it is stable enough to be useful.
Comparing to your own baselineCount review findings per 100 lines for AI-assisted code and for your own. The comparison is more informative than the absolute number, and it is often less flattering to your own code than expected.
The design-first experimentImplement the same feature twice — once stating the design first, once just describing the goal. Compare the review findings. This validates or refutes the rule rather than assuming it.
What I do
  1. Categorise every review finding from the AI-assisted feature.
  2. Count findings per 100 lines and compare with your own recent code.
  3. Run the design-first versus goal-only experiment on the same feature.
  4. Record which produced fewer and less serious findings.
What I conclude
  • The defect-category distribution is a stable, actionable finding — it tells you where to focus review attention.
  • Testing the design-first rule rather than assuming it is the intellectually honest move.
What I remember & apply
Track AI defect categories → apply: missing edge cases and error handling usually dominate.
Compare findings per 100 lines against your own code → apply: it calibrates how much extra review is warranted.
Test the design-first rule experimentally → apply: do not adopt a practice on assertion alone.
