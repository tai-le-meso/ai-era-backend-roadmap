What I learn
AI as a first-pass reviewerThe source roadmap's list is the right scope: bugs, concurrency issues, security risks, performance problems, maintainability. Advisory only — an AI review must never be a merge gate on its own, because its false-positive rate is high enough to block good work.
Building the review prompt from your conventionsA review prompt that includes your conventions file finds convention violations and stays on topic. A generic 'review this code' prompt produces style opinions nobody asked for.
Precision and the ignore thresholdIf more than roughly a third of comments are noise, engineers stop reading them — and then the useful ones are lost too. Measuring precision against your own review of the same commits is how you tune it to stay below that threshold.
What AI review cannot seeArchitectural context (does this belong here), historical context (why the code is like this), and the organisational context (this pattern was deliberately chosen). A locally-correct change that violates the architecture is invisible to it.
What I do
  1. Build the AI review prompt from your conventions file.
  2. Run it on 20 recent commits and record every comment.
  3. Review the same 20 yourself and compare.
  4. Compute precision and recall for the AI reviewer.
What I conclude
  • An AI reviewer with low precision destroys its own usefulness by training people to ignore it.
  • Architectural violations are the category it cannot see, and they are often the most important.
What I remember & apply
AI review is advisory, never a merge gate alone → apply: high false-positive rate makes it unsuitable as a blocker.
Build the review prompt from your conventions → apply: generic review prompts produce style noise.
Keep precision above the ignore threshold → apply: roughly two-thirds useful is the practical floor.
AI review cannot see architectural or historical context → apply: those checks stay human.
