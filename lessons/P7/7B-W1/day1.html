Learn I — Requirements & architecture with AI

AI-Native SDLC & Engineering Judgment · Phase 7 — Agents & AI-Native Dev · ~4.5h
Day 1 — Learn IDay 2Day 3Day 4↑ Week overview
What I learn
AI for requirement analysisGive it a vague request and ask for: implicit requirements, ambiguities needing clarification, edge cases, negative scenarios, and the questions to ask stakeholders. It is genuinely good at breadth here — it generates the checklist you would have assembled slowly.
Gherkin acceptance criteriaGiven/When/Then forces concrete scenarios. Models produce these well from a description, and the generated set is a good starting point that you then prune and correct — the pruning is where your judgment enters.
Architecture brainstorming, correctly framedAsk for three options with explicit trade-off tables, not for 'the best architecture'. Option generation is a breadth task; option selection needs context the model does not have.
What AI structurally cannot knowYour organisation's history, why the last attempt failed, which team owns what, the political constraints, your actual scale, and what your customers complain about. Every one of those changes the right answer.
What I do
  1. Take a real vague feature request from work or invent one.
  2. Run AI-assisted analysis: requirements, ambiguities, edge cases, stakeholder questions.
  3. Ask for three architecture options with trade-off tables.
  4. Write your own list of requirements before reading AI's, so you can compare honestly.
What I conclude
  • AI supplies breadth on requirement analysis; the value is in what it reminds you to consider.
  • Framing the request as 'three options with trade-offs' produces far better output than 'what should I build'.
  • The things AI cannot know are exactly the things that determine the decision.
What I remember & apply
Ask for options with trade-offs, never for 'the best' → apply: option generation is breadth, selection is context.
Write your own list first, then compare → apply: otherwise you anchor on AI's list.
AI cannot know history, politics, ownership or your customers → apply: those are your irreplaceable inputs.
Generated Gherkin is a starting set to prune → apply: the pruning is where judgment shows.
