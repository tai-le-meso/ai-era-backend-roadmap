What I learn
Arguing against event-driven architectureThere are real arguments: operational complexity, debugging difficulty, eventual-consistency surprises for users, and the fact that a modular monolith with in-process events gets much of the benefit at a fraction of the cost. A senior engineer can make this case even while choosing event-driven.
Both sides in the ADRWriting the counter-argument into the ADR is what makes it credible. An ADR that only lists reasons for the decision reads as justification, not analysis.
What I do
  1. Finish and tag v2.
  2. Write the Reason answers with the timeline diagram.
  3. Run the AI drill and write your rebuttal; put both in the ADR.
  4. Complete the Phase 2 exit checklist and do the monthly review.
What I conclude
  • Reason (write it out): write out the dual-write failure with a timeline diagram. Where does choreography make your system undebuggable? What would push you to orchestration?
  • AI drill: 'Give me the strongest argument against event-driven architecture for this system.' Then write your rebuttal. Both go into ADR-0005.
  • An ADR containing the strongest counter-argument is far more persuasive than one that only argues for the decision.
What I remember & apply
I can explain the dual-write problem in 60 seconds with a diagram → apply: the self-check for this week.
Deliverable: v2 tagged + ADR-0005 + event-flow diagram → apply: Phase 2 complete; monthly review and re-score now.
Put the counter-argument in the ADR → apply: standing practice for all 12+ ADRs in this roadmap.
